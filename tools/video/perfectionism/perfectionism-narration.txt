You've moved this logo two pixels to the left. Then two pixels back. Forty-seven times.

That's perfectionism. And it's one of the most common problems designers face.

It looks like this. A file called "logo, final, final, version twelve." A portfolio that's said "coming soon" for eight months. Scrolling through other people's best work, then deleting your own. A client waiting three extra days, for details they never even notice.

Here's the truth. The last twenty percent of polish often takes as long as everything else. And almost nobody can see it. Clients don't pay for the pixels you moved. They pay for work that's finished.

So here's how to fix it. One: give every task a time limit. When the time is up, pick your best version. Two: aim for great, not perfect. Eighty percent done and shipped beats a hundred percent stuck on your screen. Three: ship it, then improve it. Every great design has a version two. And four: compare your work to your own from last year, not to someone else's best.
